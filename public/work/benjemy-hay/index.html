the map is alive

The word means alive, and it means neighbourhood. After the HAY Live Experience at AINA, the visuals for Benjemy's HAY set grew from that double meaning: a map that breathes. 86 concepts, each one written as a GLSL shader and rendered from code: the material of the live set.

Three materials

The survey

A real neighbourhood, measured. Its blocks and voids, a strict grid, tiny reading labels. The cold half of the image.

The living

The snake of Benjemy's identity inhabits the survey. It sheds, breathes and moves through the streets. The warm half, and the only place the music enters.

The writing

Arabic letters are not a title laid on top. They are a material, like chrome and like the map: the city is seen through them.

Seven rules

  1. The material is realSnake, olive and tile come from the official files, every rebuild measured against them. The map comes from a real neighbourhood, never an invented maze.
  2. One material per imageLine, chrome, halftone or map. Never two at once.
  3. A single red eventOne line, one cell, one front. Two would no longer be an event.
  4. The grid holds everythingFixed margins, a wireframe border, tiny reading marks. The more the image moves, the stiller the grid.
  5. Sound measures, it does not shakeThe kick moves a structure: a front, a density, a thickness. No full-frame flash, no camera shake.
  6. The image keeps the trace of its calculationGrain, delay, visible survey lines. It is generated, and it shows.
  7. The black breathesAt least half the frame stays empty. In the room, the void is what makes the sign readable.
